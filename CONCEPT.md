\# Project Pursuit



\## Concept



\*\*Project Pursuit\*\* is a competition discovery and recommendation platform for high-school students.



The initial goal is to help \*\*SHSID international high-school students\*\* find academic competitions that match their interests, skill level, location, timeline, and college-application goals.



The broader long-term goal is to become a verified competition intelligence platform for high-school students in China and eventually worldwide.



\## Core Problem



Competitive high-school students often want to participate in contests, competitions, research programs, and academic events to develop their interests and strengthen their college applications.



However, competition information is fragmented. Students often rely on school announcements, Excel files, PDFs, counselor advice, friend recommendations, scattered websites, or random online lists.



This creates several problems:



\* Students may miss competitions that fit them.

\* Local and regional competitions are harder to discover than famous international ones.

\* Competition details may be outdated, incomplete, or difficult to compare.

\* Students may not know which competitions are appropriate for their level.

\* Students may not understand the relative college-admissions value of different competitions.



\## Initial Scope



Project Pursuit will start with a narrow and practical user group:



\*\*SHSID international high-school students.\*\*



The first version can focus on competitions from:



\* SHSID-recommended competition lists

\* School-provided Excel or PDF documents

\* Official competition websites linked by the school

\* Major international competitions relevant to SHSID students

\* China-based or Shanghai-based competitions that are not well-covered by existing platforms



The project may later expand to:



1\. Other international schools in Shanghai

2\. International-school students across China

3\. Chinese high-school students more generally

4\. Global high-school students



\## Why This Scope Makes Sense



Starting with SHSID is useful because:



\* The target users are clear.

\* The student need is real and personal.

\* The school already provides partial competition information.

\* The existing school materials are not optimized for search, filtering, comparison, or recommendation.

\* Regional and China-specific competitions are underrepresented on broader global platforms.

\* The project can begin with a manageable dataset instead of trying to cover every competition worldwide.



\## Differentiation



Project Pursuit should not be just another generic competition directory.



Its main differentiators should be:



\### 1. Local and School-Specific Support



Many platforms focus on famous international competitions. Project Pursuit should give stronger support to competitions relevant to SHSID students, Shanghai students, and China-based international students.



\### 2. Verified Competition Information



Competition data should be grounded in official sources whenever possible.



Important fields such as deadlines, eligibility, location, registration links, team requirements, and fees should be source-backed and regularly checked.



\### 3. AI-Assisted Recommendation



Instead of only letting users browse competitions manually, Project Pursuit should help users discover suitable competitions automatically.



The recommendation system can consider:



\* Subject interest

\* Grade level

\* Skill level

\* Location

\* Deadline feasibility

\* Online/offline format

\* Team or individual format

\* Student goals

\* Possible college-application value



\### 4. Admissions-Oriented Comparison



Project Pursuit should help students understand the possible college-application relevance of competitions.



This should not be presented as a guaranteed admissions boost. Instead, the platform can provide structured indicators such as:



\* Selectivity

\* Recognition level

\* Relevance to intended major

\* Difficulty

\* Depth of preparation required

\* Alignment with student profile

\* Potential activity-list value



\## Product Direction



The product should behave less like a static list and more like a personalized competition menu.



Users should be able to:



\* Browse competitions

\* Search by keyword

\* Filter by subject, deadline, difficulty, grade level, and location

\* View verified competition details

\* Save competitions to a personal shortlist

\* Compare competitions

\* Receive AI-assisted recommendations

\* Understand why a competition may or may not fit them

\* Track upcoming deadlines



\## Data Strategy



The initial dataset can come from:



\* SHSID competition Excel files

\* SHSID competition PDF files

\* Official competition websites

\* Public school recommendation pages

\* Major international competition websites

\* Manually submitted competitions

\* Carefully reviewed regional competition sources



Each competition record should ideally include:



\* Competition name

\* Official website

\* Subject area

\* Target students

\* Eligibility

\* Location

\* Online/offline format

\* Registration deadline

\* Competition date

\* Cost

\* Difficulty level

\* Recognition level

\* Admissions relevance notes

\* Source link

\* Last verified date

\* Confidence level



\## Reliability Principle



Project Pursuit should prioritize correctness over quantity.



If a competition detail is uncertain, the platform should clearly mark it as uncertain instead of guessing.



Possible confidence labels:



\* Verified

\* Partially verified

\* Historical information only

\* Unverified

\* Needs review



Important information such as deadlines and eligibility should be treated carefully because incorrect information can directly affect students.



\## MVP Features



The first version should stay focused.



Possible MVP features:



\* Competition database

\* Search and filters

\* Competition detail pages

\* Source links

\* Last-verified labels

\* Basic user preference form

\* AI-assisted recommendation

\* Saved shortlist

\* Deadline display

\* Admin interface for adding or editing competitions



Features that can wait:



\* Social features

\* Public user profiles

\* School-wide accounts

\* Counselor dashboards

\* Mobile app

\* Payment system

\* Large-scale global scraping

\* Complex admissions scoring



\## Long-Term Vision



Project Pursuit can gradually become a broader platform for high-school academic opportunity discovery.



Possible future expansions include:



\* More schools

\* More regions

\* Research programs

\* Summer programs

\* Hackathons

\* Scholarships

\* Essay contests

\* Olympiads

\* Local competitions

\* Team-finding features

\* Deadline reminders

\* Counselor-facing tools

\* Application planning tools



\## Core Principle



Project Pursuit should not simply collect more competitions.



It should help students find the right competitions, understand why they fit, verify the important details, and avoid missing meaningful opportunities.
